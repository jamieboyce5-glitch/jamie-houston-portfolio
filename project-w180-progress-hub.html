WORK180: Progress Hub

An employer portal that empowers users to manage their profile data, complete the DEI Assessment, access the knowledge base, and use product features like the gender bias analyser.

RoleLead Product Designer
CompanyWORK180
Year2022–23
NDAAvailable on request

Overview

Our existing employer portal didn't support the updated DEI assessment model and offered only a limited self-service experience. Employers struggled to manage their own data independently, leading to frequent support requests that diverted valuable time from Account Managers.

The objective was to design a more intuitive, scalable portal that would enable employers with clearer insights and actions, incorporating the new DEI assessment, reducing the time Account Managers spent assisting clients, and significantly cutting the time it takes new employers to launch their profile by 50%.

The challenge

The existing portal created friction at every stage of the employer onboarding journey. Through early user testing and feedback, Account Managers always received [COMPLETE THIS SENTENCE].

No self-service access

Employers couldn't access or update their own profile data independently, creating a bottleneck through Account Managers.

Confusing DEI assessment

The DEI assessment was a fully manual process run through spreadsheets. We needed to bring it into the portal, make it intuitive to complete, and allow employers to save and return at any time to track their progress.

No progress visibility

There was no way to track progress across different states and requirements, making the process feel opaque and unmanageable.

Manual save and submit

Saving, submitting, and editing information required manual AM support, slowing onboarding and increasing operational overhead.

The primary business goal was to increase employer profile launch rates by 50% and enable a fully self-service experience, reducing Account Manager contact hours.

Process

I joined the product team as UX Designer mid-way through this project, working closely with the Senior UX Designer to design wireframes and user flows for key features, tailoring experiences based on user entitlements.

I helped plan design workshops and tested designs with internal stakeholders and a select group of employers to validate usability and influence ongoing design decisions. Throughout delivery, I collaborated closely with engineers to ensure a smooth handoff, documenting interaction patterns, edge cases, and providing ongoing support to maintain quality.

Since taking over as lead designer, I've been responsible for continuous refinements informed by real usage data from our endorsed employers, re-branding the portal and expanding the design system with new and refined components.

Key takeaways

01

Design for empowerment

Good UX reduces reliance on manual support. When users can confidently self-serve, it frees up the whole team.

02

Collaboration is critical

Early and ongoing alignment with Product, Engineering, and stakeholders kept the project on track and reduced rework.

03

Balance complexity and clarity

When designing for compliance-heavy workflows, clear guidance and progressive disclosure are essential to reducing cognitive load.
