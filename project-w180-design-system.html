WORK180:
Design System

A comprehensive design system and component library built for scale, covering colour, typography, spacing, and a full component suite used across WORK180's employer-facing and job seeker products.

Design Systems Component Library UI Design Figma Brand
Overview

The challenge

WORK180 didn't have a formalised design system when I joined. Components were inconsistent across surfaces, colour usage was inconsistent especially with the company going through two re-brands, and there was no shared Figma library. I was tasked with building a scalable system from the ground up, one that could serve both the employer portal and job seeker product, and evolve alongside the business.

Process

How I built it

I audited the existing product to catalogue every UI pattern in use, then worked with the engineering and brand teams to align on tokens, naming conventions, and contribution guidelines. The system was built iteratively, starting with foundations (colour, type, spacing) and expanding into components as new product work demanded them.

Audit & inventory
Catalogued all existing UI patterns across the employer portal and job seeker product to identify inconsistencies and gaps.
Token definition
Defined semantic colour tokens, type scale, and spacing system with engineering alignment on naming conventions.
Component build
Built reusable Figma components with variants, auto-layout, and documented usage guidelines for each.
Documentation
Created usage guidelines, do/don't examples, and accessibility notes.
Iteration & adoption
Rolled the system into active product work, refining components based on real usage and team feedback.

I'm a product designer with 10 years across freelance, in-house, and startup environments. My background spans product strategy, UX research, UI design, branding, and illustration so I can carry an idea from a whiteboard conversation all the way to a shipped, on-brand interface.

I'm currently a Product Designer at WORK180 in Melbourne, working end-to-end on the employer-facing platform from discovery and research through to delivery and iteration.

Because I don't just design screens I think about outcomes. I started in visual and brand design, which means I care deeply about craft and polish. I grew into UX and product, which means I care about whether it actually works for people and moves the business.

I'm also fluent with AI-enabled workflows and front-end enough to communicate precisely with engineers. I move fast without dropping quality, and I thrive in teams where design and engineering are genuinely collaborative.

Discovery first I want to understand the problem, the users, and the constraints before I sketch a single screen. That means research, stakeholder conversations, and auditing what already exists.

Then I move fast in low-fidelity: flows, rough wireframes, quick prototypes for testing. High-fidelity comes after we've validated the approach, not before. And I stay involved post-launch data and user feedback inform the next iteration.

As a partner, not a handoff machine. I try to involve engineers early ideally in problem framing so they're invested in the solution, not just implementing a spec.

I'm comfortable reviewing implementations for design fidelity and adjusting specs when engineering constraints make something impractical. I'd rather ship something slightly simpler and well-built than something pixel-perfect and brittle.

The challenge
We identified an opportunity to help young people find the right apprenticeship path with full transparency, while giving employers a way to manage applications on the other side. The tricky part was that it's a two-sided product with very different user needs, and we jumped straight into it as an experiment with almost no runway. I had a week to show a working prototype to a potential client, starting from scratch.

What I did
I ran discovery and scoped the project myself, mapping out both sides of the experience simultaneously. I had to make fast, pragmatic calls about what to explore and what to park, and built the prototype end-to-end to get something tangible in front of stakeholders quickly.

How it evolved
The whole process has been learning while building, taking feedback from research and changing direction based on what we found rather than following a fixed plan. That's been the real design challenge: staying structured enough to make progress while staying loose enough to respond to what we learn.

Where it landed
We're currently in validation with some strong early signals. It hasn't shipped yet but the process itself validated a lot of our assumptions about what both sides of the market actually need.

Range, and where I sit between disciplines. I started in visual and brand design, so I have a strong eye for typography, layout, and polish but I grew into UX, design systems, and front-end, so I can carry an idea from a stakeholder conversation all the way to a built, accessible, on-brand interface.

That blend matters for work that lives between marketing, communications, and product. I'm also a systems thinker who still sweats the pixels.
